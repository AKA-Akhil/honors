## 1. Setup
- **Models** (all quantized locally from one F16 GGUF, one recipe): Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct. Sources: bartowski GGUF repos (F16 for Llama and Qwen; Phi has only F32, which was converted to F16 with `llama-quantize`; see `results/model_provenance.csv`, `DECISIONS.md`).
- **F16 SHA256**: Llama `b5ebe8fb…fb8ea6cd`, Qwen `25908e40…d140aa5032`, Phi (converted) `062a92a0…ec32864` (full hashes in `results/model_provenance.csv`).
- **Tooling**: llama.cpp release b11972 (`llama-imatrix`, `llama-quantize`, `llama-perplexity`); inference with llama-cpp-python 0.3.36 (its bundled llama.cpp differs from b11972, a threat to validity, see 6).
- **Imatrix**: WikiText-2 train, 100 chunks, ctx 512. Levels Q8_0, Q6_K, Q5_K_M, Q4_K_M, Q3_K_M, Q2_K. No-imatrix ablation at Q4_K_M, Q3_K_M, Q2_K (template A only). Perplexity: WikiText-2 test, 50 chunks, ctx 512.
- **Data**: 150 synthetic resumes x 4 tiers, 4 name-swapped variants each (600 variants), 3 prompt templates -> 1,800 prompts per run; 72 runs (3 models x (7 levels x 3 templates + 3 ablation runs)). Score = P(Yes) from the full-vocabulary next-token softmax (yes/no mass is ~1.0 in every main (F16/imatrix) run, see `yesno_mass.csv`; the exceptions are two Q2_K no-imatrix ablation runs: Phi 5.3% of prompts below 0.5 and **Qwen 100% below 0.5, i.e. a genuine format failure there**).
- **Hardware**: RTX 4050 Laptop (6 GB), 14 cores, 16 GB RAM, Windows 11. F16 files (6.4-7.6 GB) ran with partial GPU offload (auto-tuned, `DECISIONS.md`). Determinism check (100 prompts, Q4_K_M and F16, every model): max |diff| = 0.0.
- **Reference** is F16 (round 1 used Q8_0). Statistics: resume-level bootstrap (2,000 resamples), Wilcoxon, McNemar, Holm within families (`results/tables/families.md`), crossed mixed models.
- Free disk after cleanup: see STATUS.md.

## 2. What changed from round 1, and do the three round-1 findings hold?
Changes: local quantization with a single recipe (round 1 mixed unsloth/bartowski files), F16 reference, 3 models instead of 2, 150 resumes instead of 50, imatrix ablation, mixed models, flip/uncertainty analysis, verification script, corrected citations (`results/citation_check.md`).

1. *"Gaps stay tiny with no trend toward larger gaps at lower precision."* **Holds.** Largest |gender gap| for any F16/imatrix run is 0.017 (Qwen B, Q5_K_M: -0.017); changes versus F16 are at most ~0.01 and mostly CIs spanning zero. Exceptions are small and listed in 3.2.
2. *"Individual decisions change a lot."* **Holds for Qwen, not for Llama/Phi at >=Q4_K_M.** Qwen flips 29% (Q3_K_M) and 37% (Q2_K) of decisions vs F16, all Yes->No. Llama flips 0-2% and Phi 0-1% down to Q4_K_M; Phi Q2_K 7%. Round 1's "up to 26.5%" came from one model and a Q8_0 reference.
3. *"Ranking survives, calibration shifts, depends on prompt wording."* **Partly.** Ranking for strong vs weak survives everywhere (AUC 1.0) but adjacent tiers do not always (3.1), and several effects are template-specific (3.4).

## 3. Results by research question
### 3.1 Capability / calibration
Perplexity (WikiText-2 test, imatrix): ratio to F16 is <=1.02 through Q4_K_M for Llama and Phi, 1.06 (Llama) or 1.08 (Phi) at Q3_K_M and 1.41 (Llama) / 1.43 (Phi) at Q2_K. Qwen is erratic: Q3_K_M ppl 12.78 (1.55x) but Q2_K 11.07 (1.34x); without imatrix Qwen Q2_K collapses (ppl 19,563).
Selection rate at 0.5 (CI in tables): Llama 0.527 (F16) -> 0.510-0.548 across levels; Phi 0.613 -> 0.610-0.623, Q2_K 0.683; Qwen 0.373 -> 0.385, 0.388, 0.318, 0.355, **0.083 (Q3_K_M), 0.000 (Q2_K)**.
Adjacent-tier AUC (A): Llama borderline-high vs borderline-low 0.99 at all levels, strong vs borderline-high degrades 0.86 -> 0.74 (Q3_K_M, Q2_K). Phi Q2_K breaks down (borderline-high vs low 0.84 vs 0.997 at F16; strong vs borderline-high 0.46). Qwen's weak vs borderline-low AUC is unstable (0.65-0.87 at Q8_0-Q4_K_M, 0.99 at Q3_K_M/Q2_K, where almost everything is "No").
### 3.2 Bias (paired gaps, change vs F16)
- Llama has a consistent small female advantage already at F16 (gender gap M-F = -0.008, Holm p<0.001, all templates), unchanged by quantization (changes within +/-0.003; only Q6_K nominally excludes zero, not Holm-significant).
- Qwen has a small White>Black gap at F16 (+0.010, A; Holm p=0.001) not present in B/C; Phi gaps are ~0.
- Holm-significant changes vs F16 (imatrix, A): only Llama Q4_K_M race gap (+0.0026, template-specific) and Phi Q2_K race gap (-0.006). Qwen Q3_K_M/Q2_K race-gap changes have CIs excluding zero in A (-0.011, -0.009) but those are pooled with near-total "No" outputs (selection 0-8%), so gaps shrink mechanically; not evidence of reduced bias.
- No gender or race gap grows materially with quantization in any model. Mixed models: all level x demographic interaction terms are non-significant after Holm; 5 of 9 original fits had not converged; after refitting with other optimizers all 9 converge and no conclusion changes (section 8, Task 4).
### 3.3 Flips and uncertainty
Flip rate vs F16 (A): Llama 0.0, 0.3, 0.7, 1.7, 1.2, 2.2%; Qwen 1.2, 1.8, 5.5, 3.2, **29.0, 37.3%**; Phi 0.0, 0.0, 0.3, 0.2, 1.0, 7.0% (Q8_0 -> Q2_K). Direction: Llama Q4_K_M Yes->No (10 vs 0), Llama/Phi Q2_K No->Yes; Qwen overwhelmingly Yes->No. **No flip-direction/name-group test is significant after Holm (0 of 63)**: flips are not concentrated on any demographic group.
Flips concentrate where F16 was uncertain: logit odds ratio per 0.1 of |P(yes)-0.5| is 0.23-0.47 across all 9 model-templates (Holm p<=0.025), consistent with Hua et al.'s uncertainty finding, now for hiring.
### 3.4 Imatrix ablation and template robustness
- Imatrix matters mainly at Q2_K/Q3_K_M. Llama Q2_K: selection +22 pp without imatrix vs +2 pp with, flip rate 22% vs 2%. Qwen Q4_K_M: -6.3 vs -1.8 pp; Qwen Q3_K_M: both collapse (-13.8 vs -29.0 pp, different degenerate behavior). Phi: no significant difference. Bias-gap changes differ between recipes only for Llama Q3_K_M/Q2_K and are small (<=0.009).
- Robustness (selection shift): robust across all 3 templates = Qwen Q5_K_M, Q3_K_M, Q2_K; partly robust = Llama Q2_K (templates disagree in sign); 6 template-specific; 8 not significant. Gender-gap changes: 0 of 18 significant in any template. Race-gap changes: 2 template-specific, 16 not significant. Cross-template correlations of gap changes are weak or negative (e.g. gender_gap_change A vs B r=-0.46), i.e. gap change is mostly noise.

## 4. Key findings
1. **Demographic gaps are not made worse by quantization** in any of the three models down to Q2_K; baseline gaps (Llama pro-female ~0.8 pp, Qwen small White>Black under template A) are inherited from F16.
2. **Decision stability is model-dependent, not level-dependent alone**: Llama and Phi are essentially stable to Q4_K_M; Qwen2.5-3B shifts at Q5_K_M already (-5.5 pp, 33 Yes->No flips) and collapses at Q3_K_M.
3. **Flips track F16 uncertainty** (OR 0.23-0.47 per 0.1) and are demographically balanced.
4. **Perplexity tracks drift within a model but does not predict its size across models**: pooled Spearman rho = 0.73 (selection shift) / 0.77 (flip rate), yet Qwen Q3_K_M (+55% perplexity, 29% flips) versus Llama (+5.6%, 1.2%) and Phi (+8.0%, 1.0%) shows the same level costing very different decisions; Qwen's Q2_K has lower ppl than Q3_K_M yet more drift. Imatrix helps most at low bits but not uniformly.
6. **Qwen's collapse at Q3_K_M/Q2_K (imatrix) is a genuine "No" shift, not a format failure** (section 8, Task 1); only the no-imatrix Q2_K file breaks instruction following.
5. **Many apparent effects are template-specific**; only the Qwen selection shifts are robust to wording.

## 5. Suggested paper framing and unsupported claims
Frame as "stable bias gaps, model-dependent decision drift". **Do not claim**: that quantization increases or decreases hiring bias; that results hold for other model families or sizes or real resumes; that imatrix always helps; any causal link to perplexity; Qwen's gap shrinkage at Q3_K_M/Q2_K as a bias improvement. Do not describe Qwen's collapse as an instruction-following failure for the imatrix files; do not use the Qwen no-imatrix Q2_K row as evidence of anything except a broken model (ppl ~2e4, ~0 yes/no mass). Revise round-1 paper claims that conflict with 2 (Q8_0 reference, flip magnitudes) and fix citations per `results/citation_check.md`.

## 6. Threats to validity
Synthetic resumes and name proxies for race/gender; only 3 models of ~3-4B parameters; one quantization toolchain; inference engine (llama-cpp-python 0.3.36 bundled llama.cpp) differs from b11972 used for quantization/perplexity; partial GPU offload for F16 (determinism checked, diff 0.0); perplexity from 50 chunks (SE 2-3%); the 0.5 threshold is arbitrary (P(Yes) results are also reported); mixed-model convergence resolved in the final checks (section 8); Qwen no-imatrix Q2_K degenerate (ppl ~2e4, selection 0%).

### Task 2: Does perplexity predict decision drift?
`perplexity_vs_drift.csv`, `perplexity_vs_drift_spearman.csv`, Figure 7 (annotated with rho). Template A; relative perplexity increase vs F16 against |selection shift| and flip rate.
- Pooled over the 18 imatrix model-levels: Spearman rho = 0.73 (p = 0.001) for |selection shift| and 0.77 (p < 0.001) for flip rate. Excluding Qwen: 0.80 / 0.88. Excluding only Qwen Q2_K and Q3_K_M: 0.63 / 0.70. Within a model, rho is 0.66-0.94 (n = 6 each; Llama selection shift p = 0.16).
- **Qwen Q3_K_M vs the others at the same level**: perplexity +54.7% (Qwen) vs +5.6% (Llama) and +8.0% (Phi); flips 29.0% vs 1.2% and 1.0%; selection shift -29.0 pp vs +0.2 and +1.0 pp. At Q2_K perplexity increases are similar across models (+34%, +41%, +43%) but flips are 37%, 2% and 7%.
- **Verdict**: perplexity ranks levels within a model sensibly (more perplexity, more drift) but does not predict the magnitude of drift across models. With 18 points and strong level/model confounding, rho is descriptive.

### Task 3: llama.cpp version consistency
Bundled in llama-cpp-python 0.3.36: ggml 0.25.3, commit 0c1e570 (no llama.cpp build number is exposed). Release b11972 reports "0.8.0-dev (build 11972, commit cf24317be)". The b11972 CUDA `llama-server` scored 100 seeded template-A prompts on Qwen F16 (ngl 24) and Q2_K (imatrix), using the same chat template and the same pre-computed token ids; p_yes = P(yes ids)/(P(yes ids)+P(no ids)) over the top-300 returned probabilities (yes/no mass ~1.0, so the truncation is immaterial). `results/version_consistency.csv`:

| Level | n | max abs diff | mean abs diff | identical decisions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F16 | 100 | 0.056 | 0.0027 | 100% |
| Q2_K | 100 | 0.061 | 0.0032 | 100% |

The differences come from kernels and offload and change no decision. This supports the claim that round-2 decisions do not depend on the engine version for these two files; it does not cover other models.

### Task 4: Mixed-effects models
`mixed_methods.csv`, `mixed_effects_final.csv`. A fallback ladder (lbfgs, bfgs, powell, nm, cg at 3000 iterations; centred/standardised variant; resume-only random effect; OLS with resume-clustered SEs) was prepared. **All 9 fits converge with the original specification (crossed resume + first-name variance components, treatment coding): lbfgs for 3 (Llama B, Qwen A, Qwen C), Powell for the other 6.** The OLS fallback was not needed. Joint Wald p-values for gender x level, race x level and three-way terms are 0.85-1.00 in all 9 fits; no coefficient is Holm-significant; no conclusion changes. The earlier "5 of 9 not converged" caveat is resolved.

### Task 5: Undefined gaps
`selection_by_group.csv` now sets impact ratio, the four-fifths flag and the new selection-rate gender/race gaps to undefined (blank) whenever any of the four groups has a selection rate of exactly 0% or 100% (`ratio_status`). This affects 11 of 144 rows (Qwen Q2_K/Q3_K_M at 0.5 threshold, Phi Q2_K at the F16-median threshold, Qwen no-imatrix Q2_K), which previously showed 0 or 1 and `below_four_fifths = False`. Paired P(Yes) gaps are continuous and stay defined, but at Qwen Q3_K_M/Q2_K they shrink mechanically because nearly all P(Yes) values are ~0, so they are not evidence of reduced bias. Remaining NaNs in other tables are tests with no discordant pairs or no baseline.
